India Defence Shipbuilding — Structural Upcycle
India's Navy is in the most aggressive expansion in its history. CNS announced 15 warship inductions in 2026 (vs average 4–6 historically). Target: 200+ ship force by 2035, all built in Indian shipyards. Total defence capital procurement in FY26: ₹9.28 trn AoN clearances — a 3.4× YoY jump. FY27 BE defence capex +22% to ₹2,193 bn = 27.9% of total defence budget (highest since FY15). ₹2.38 lakh Cr mega-procurement cleared in March 2026, accelerated post-Operation Sindoor (May 2025). MDL's addressable pipeline: P75I (₹80,000 Cr) + P75-AS (₹40,000 Cr) + P17B (₹70,000 Cr) + LPD (₹40,000 Cr) + MCMV (₹40,000 Cr) + P15C destroyer (₹80,000 Cr) = ₹3.5 lakh Cr over 10 years. MDL monopoly on complex combatants (submarines + destroyers) is structurally protected. Global shipbuilding super-cycle (CAGR +8.6% past 3 years vs long-term +4%) provides tailwinds for commercial exports via Colombo Dockyard.

MDL read-through: higher government borrowing yields and weak market breadth raise the importance of order award timing, cash conversion and capex discipline; the company’s defence demand is budget-linked, but a policy pipeline is not contracted revenue. The 23 Sep P75(I) report indicates Indian approvals remain pending.
Competitive Landscape — Who Else Can Build These Ships?
Submarines: MDL is the only yard. P75I alternatives (foreign build) were eliminated by Atmanirbhar policy. Destroyers: MDL (P15B, P17A) + GRSE (OPVs, frigates) — GRSE cannot build destroyers. Frigates: MDL (P17A) + GRSE (P17 class); P17B will be competitive bid but MDL track record is decisive. Corvettes: GRSE + Garden Reach + MDL — lower end. OPVs/Patrol Vessels: Cochin Shipyard, GRSE, L&T Shipbuilding — MDL doesn't compete here. MDL's moat: The destroyer-submarine nexus is unreplicable for 5–8 years. Private yards (L&T Shipbuilding, Swan Defence) are scaling but winning patrol vessels and LPDs — not attacking MDL's core. Cochin Shipyard (P/E 63×) competes on corvettes and survey vessels; recent NGSV L1 win shows it is growing. MDL's discount to Cochin (37.7× vs 63×) is driven by order book timing, not franchise quality.
MDL's Sub-Archetype Characteristics (Shipyard PSU)
Shipyard PSU economics differ from other defence PSUs: Capital-intensive, multi-year S-curve revenue recognition, negative working capital (MoD advances), and violent OPM swings at project completion. Key metrics to track (not standard P&L): (1) Order book/revenue ratio — must stay above 2.5× for sustained growth. (2) Project completion stage — margin spikes at completion are normal, not sustainable. (3) Customer advance balance — declining advance (contract liability fell 33.5% YoY in Q4 FY26) means existing projects are closing out; need fresh advances for new orders. (4) Sub-contracting % — MDL optimising in-house vs sub-contracting for complex systems. (5) Retentions/liquidated damages — ₹102 Cr LD waived Q2 FY26 (P17A 2nd ship); more waivers expected on submarine project.
