Operating model and market position
Aequs is an engineering-led contract manufacturer across aerospace and consumer manufacturing, not an aircraft OEM or defence prime. Its Belagavi SEZ integrates forging, precision machining, surface treatment and assembly. Aerospace remains its larger, profitable segment; consumer electronics, toys, plastics, cookware/durables are the second platform and the source of current group losses. Customer qualifications and long program life support switching costs but concentrate sales with a limited number of OEMs.
Aerospace cycles, capacity and competitive context
FY26 annual report: aerospace segment revenue ₹1,046.4 Cr (+27%), EBITDA ₹281.3 Cr (+76%) and segment ROCE 20%; FY26 ending order book USD889m. Q1 FY27 backlog USD1.004bn and 5,740 parts, with utilization 70% total / 78% India. Q1 wins increase opportunity but Safran order value and the backlog delivery curve are not disclosed. New Hosur aerospace ecosystem expects revenue from FY29. The competitive edge is integrated process depth and OEM qualification, balanced against long machine lead times, imported qualified alloys and customer build-rate exposure.
Defence exposure and index classification
BSE official India Defence index (code 155) lists Aequs Limited, BSE code 544634, classified Industrials; index methodology includes stocks with a defence association. FY26 annual report says Ajna Aerospace & Defence was formed with Aequs, Accel and Vagus Defence to pursue UAVs primarily for Indian defence; the Jan 2026 JV disclosure states equal intended shareholding, and Mar 2026 filing records Aequs investment of ₹10.01 Cr. Q4 FY26 management described the US location as doing low-volume ITAR defence work. No separate defence revenue, order book, materiality, or JV contribution is published. Aerospace revenue is not booked as defence for this analysis.
Consumer turnaround and capital allocation
Consumer revenue ₹184 Cr in FY26 (+84%) and ₹73.4 Cr Q1 FY27 (+190% YoY); consumer EBITDA loss ₹78.3 Cr FY26 and ₹36.1 Cr Q1 FY27. Q4FY26 disclosed Hasbro would stop raising purchase orders; management signed a long-term Mattel agreement and reiterated growth expectations. Sep 25 promoter warrants propose ₹650 Cr for aerospace/consumer capex and debt capacity; separately, FY27 capex guide is about ₹660 Cr, with FY27 5-year capex requirement USD350–400m per calls. Distinguish actual cash raised and utilization from board approval and long-range MoUs.
