This Is NOT 2022

Sajid Chinoy (JP Morgan) makes the critical distinction: In 2022, Russia-Ukraine was a pure price shock with no threat to volume availability. Today, the risk is actual physical shortage of energy volumes.

If supply rationing occurs for 6+ weeks, think of this as 2020 (COVID), not 2022 (Ukraine). Once a business shuts down, it's very hard to reverse. Once a gig worker goes home to the village, very hard to reverse. Hysteresis from quantitative constraints is far worse than price-induced adjustments.

15-20 Year Global Readjustment

Neelkanth Mishra (Axis Capital): The current conflict is part of a much longer 15-20 year global readjustment. Contributing forces:

  • → US control of supply chain choke points (Panama, Hormuz, Djibouti)
  • → China's overproduction / underconsumption imbalance
  • → Dollar dominance tensions and settlement diversification
  • → AI-driven structural disruption across economies

Raghuram Rajan adds: The energy shock compounds existing vulnerabilities — US inflation still sticky, interest rates must stay high, and many countries carry large fiscal deficits. UK gilts moved 75 bps in one month. Financial sector stress is the next cascading risk.

Is India Uniquely Resilient or Uniquely Vulnerable?

RESILIENT
Nilan Mishra (Axis Bank): "Compared to any other time in the past, we are the best prepared." Low fiscal deficits, slack, low inflation, RBI reserves. India emerges stronger post-crisis. V-shaped capex recovery underway.
VULNERABLE
Neelkanth Mishra (Axis Capital): India imports nearly 50% of dense energy. We sit in the middle of the demand destruction curve. "We'll be fairly badly impacted. But it's a one-time impact, not a permanent derailment."
Synthesis

Both are correct but on different time horizons. India IS more vulnerable near-term (3.8% GDP energy dependence) but IS structurally stronger for recovery (fiscal discipline, reserves, demographics). The question is whether crisis duration allows the structural strengths to matter.

Oil Price Direction After Ceasefire

GRADUAL
Kunal Shah (Nirmal Bang): $80 floor is the new normal. Far-month contracts already pricing normalization. Sell rallies in far-month contracts. 6-8 month field restart = gradual but certain normalization.
EXTENDED

RBI Policy Response

HIKE
Shalendra Jengar (ICICI): BOP deficit for two consecutive years demands higher rates to attract capital flows, regardless of domestic inflation. Looking at growth + inflation alone is insufficient.
HOLD
Samiran Chakraborty (Citi): Core inflation framework allows RBI to look through supply-side shocks. As long as core stays below 4.5%, no rate hike needed. RBI's core CPI publication is strategically sound.
CAUTIOUS
Mridul Saggar (ex-RBI): Brainard rule: when uncertainty is exceptionally high, central banks should move cautiously and avoid overreacting to incomplete information.
