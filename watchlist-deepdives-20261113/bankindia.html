Bank of India

Bank of India presents a low-multiple profitability recovery with strong balance-sheet expansion, but the September quarter exposes funding-mix and provision-quality questions. The discount to book may narrow if returns improve sustainably; the key is separating core spread improvement from treasury income and standard-asset provision reversals.

Research viewValuation support with earnings-quality caveats; monitor funding cost, provision reversals and ECL rather than treating cheap P/E as sufficient.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • The quarter demonstrates volume strength: global loans grew 20.58% and deposits 21.59%. Domestic figures differ, at 17.84% loan growth and 23.29% deposit growth, so not all headline balance-sheet expansion should be valued as identical domestic spread income.
  • The liability-quality question is sharper than the growth headline. CASA rose about 7% in absolute rupees, but the ratio fell from 36.68% to 34.16% as other deposits expanded faster. The economic test is whether incremental NII compensates for a more expensive mix, not whether deposits increased.

PSU-bank recovery lens

Bank of India’s franchise value should be analysed through funding costs, recoveries, credit underwriting and return on capital. Sales/EBITDA/ROCE comparisons with industrial companies are inappropriate.

Deposit mix versus deposit growth

CASA fell to 34.16% from 36.68% in Q1 despite growth in the absolute CASA balance. FCNR deposits and associated lending can expand the balance sheet while diluting NIM; aggregate growth alone does not establish better franchise economics.

Macro read-through

Loan repricing after a rate increase can aid spreads, but deposit competition and investment-book sensitivity are offsets. Higher rates can also expose borrower stress.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

Reverse valuation and sustainable-return constraints; point target withheld

The snapshot P/B of 0.74 x and P/E of 5.61 x look inexpensive, but reflect lower or less certain sustainable returns than premium peers. Provision reversals and low current credit costs warrant conservative normalisation.

  • Current reported book value is ₹207 per share in the consolidated snapshot.
  • A fair-value P/ABV requires ECL, subsidiary and unprovided NPA adjustments not completed here.
  • The previous arbitrary bear/base/bull target-price sensitivities have been removed. The deeper valuation discussion states the economic return conditions and analyst assumptions instead.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • The dated snapshot P/B is 0.74 x and ROE is 12.4%. A deliberately simplified perpetual-bank identity, P/B=(ROE −g)/(cost of equity −g), using analyst assumptions of 13% cost of equity and 6% long-run growth, would require a steady-state ROE of about 11.2% at that book multiple. This is a diagnostic of the earnings expectations in the price, not a fair value, forecast or calibrated discount rate. A changing growth phase, subsidiary values, adjusted book and surplus capital can materially alter the answer.
  • A 0.74 x snapshot book multiple is not automatically a margin of safety: the value case depends on protecting normalized ROE after the current credit-cost trough and provision releases. The stylized model’s approximately 11.2% required steady-state ROE is near the 12.4% snapshot ROE, leaving sensitivity to modest return normalization. This comparison is descriptive, not a target or an ECL-adjusted book valuation.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Union’s higher dated P/B of 1.07 x accompanies higher snapshot ROE of 15.7%, compared with BOI’s 0.74 x and 12.4%. A rerating requires evidence that BOI narrows the sustainable-return gap, not merely that both banks report low current NPAs. Private-bank multiples incorporate different liability and franchise characteristics and should not be imported mechanically.
