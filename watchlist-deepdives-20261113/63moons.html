Conservative asset-by-asset sum-of-parts; no fair value target

P/E is not meaningful with negative earnings. Snapshot book ₹760/share and P/B 1.18× are not a liquidation value. Restricted, disputed or non-cash assets must not be treated as freely distributable cash.

  • No DCF, target price or probability weighted value without a verified asset/liability and legal claims bridge.
  • Operating business value needs segment cash flows and customer-economics data.
  • P/B is a descriptive cross-check, not an evidence-backed margin of safety.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • P/E is not meaningful while owners’ earnings are negative. The dated ₹760/share book value is a starting balance-sheet descriptor, not an independently verified realizable NAV. An investable SOTP would haircut restricted/disputed assets, estimate enforceable liabilities and include future operating/subsidiary funding requirements.
  • No target price is supplied because legal and asset-recovery uncertainty can dominate the precision of an operating forecast. A convincing valuation upgrade needs either demonstrably profitable segment cash flows or a verified improvement in legal/asset accessibility; a rising share price is not such evidence.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Profitable exchange-technology vendors and cybersecurity platforms are not valid P/E peers for the entire group without isolating operating businesses from legacy investments and legal claims. Compare each subsidiary’s recurring contracts, gross margin, customer retention and cash burn separately; sum-of-parts requires the parent’s actual ownership and funding obligations.
