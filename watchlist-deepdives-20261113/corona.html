How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • The 97% India revenue mix makes doctor relationships, therapy depth, brand retention and representative productivity central. Growth excluding Wokadine of 21.5% supports the existing-franchise case; it is more informative than treating every new indication or export registration as a separate growth engine.

Reverse valuation and cash-earnings constraints; no unsupported point target

About 69× available trailing EPS is demanding even for a strong domestic prescription franchise. The 1.5× IPM growth objective and 20% PAT ambition can support earnings growth, but they do not guarantee an investment return if valuation normalises or reinvestment absorbs cash.

  • At the 12 November adjusted close of ₹2,342.20, the available four-quarter EPS sum is ₹33.85, implying approximately 69.2× earnings and a 1.45% earnings yield. This is a price/available-EPS calculation, not a forecast or a fully normalized multiple.
  • A 20% contraction in the earnings multiple would require 25% cumulative EPS growth merely to keep the share price unchanged; dividends are excluded. This is algebraic sensitivity, not a prediction of the exit multiple.
  • No terminal multiple or target is justified merely by highROCE or a therapeutic market size; acquired-brand amortisation, plant utilisation and cash returns need an explicit forecast.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • At roughly 69× available earnings, a 20% decline in the multiple requires 25% cumulative EPS growth to maintain price. Even achievement of the 20% PAT ambition over one year would not fully offset that contraction; dividends and ownership changes aside, the arithmetic explains why strong operations can still yield a modest stock return.
  • The 1.5× IPM guide is a relative growth commitment, not a fixed revenue number. A defensible valuation should test slower IPM growth, stable versus improving brand productivity, facility absorption and acquisition cash requirements. The CFO’s explicit rejection of a mid-20 s margin guide rules out using that margin as a management-backed base case.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Domestic therapy-led branded-pharma businesses are the relevant earnings-quality peers; largeAPI orU.S.generic manufacturers have different pricing and regulatory exposures. Corona’s high domestic concentration is a strength in franchise clarity, but also means international optionality should be valued separately and conservatively.
  • Compare growth excluding acquisitions, chronic mix, brand concentration, representative productivity and cash returns. Broad pharmaceuticalP/E tables alone cannot establish that Corona’s much higher earnings multiple is justified.
