How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • India 10% volume growth and international 13% CCgrowth are stronger evidence than reported revenue alone, particularly during copra deflation. Parachute’s 13% volume growth with price reductions suggests demand and share momentum, but reported share is management-attributed and channel health still needs verification.
  • The portfolio is moving toward foods, premium personal care and digital brands. Their faster growth can diversify the franchise, butARR, recognized sales, segment contribution and customer-acquisition costs differ. A larger share of new businesses does not automatically mean a higher consolidated return on capital.

Business model and sector lens

Separate copra from crude derivatives and edible oils; their price movements differ. Revenue growth under deflation can understate consumer volume, while price hikes under inflation can overstate demand. Brand strength and distribution should be evaluated with volume, share and cash generation together.

Reverse valuation and cash-earnings constraints; no unsupported point target

The available price/EPS calculation is about 58×. The cash-generating franchise and 10% India volume growth are supportive, but a commodity-assisted FY27 profit upgrade does not guarantee a higher permanent growth rate. Valuation should reflect normalized copra costs, recurringA&P needs and brand profitability rather than capitalising peak gross-margin relief.

  • At the 12 November adjusted close of ₹902.00, the available four-quarter EPS sum is ₹15.53, implying approximately 58.1× earnings and a 1.72% earnings yield. This is a price/available-EPS calculation, not a forecast or a fully normalized multiple.
  • A 20% contraction in the earnings multiple would require 25% cumulative EPS growth merely to keep the share price unchanged; dividends are excluded. This is algebraic sensitivity, not a prediction of the exit multiple.
  • No point fair value is assigned from an unverified terminal multiple or FY29 aspiration; brand-level recognized revenue and cash contribution are needed.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • At about 58× available EPS, cash quality alone does not establish an attractive entry price. A20% multiple contraction requires 25% EPS growth merely to keep price unchanged. The FY27 mid-20 s EBITDA upgrade helps, but management explicitly retains a mid-teens growth view for subsequent years.
  • The FY29 ₹20,000 cr aspiration has no full bottom-upbuild disclosed on the call. A valuation should therefore model named drivers—India volume, price/cost cycles,CCinternational growth and recognized new-brand contribution—rather than solve backward to that headline.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Large consumer-staples peers help compare pricing power, distribution, cash conversion andA&Pintensity. Yet Marico’s concentrated coconut-oil exposure and growing digital/food portfolio make an unadjusted category-average multiple a weak valuation argument.
  • Compare normalized margins through both inflation and deflation, and assess whether new brands add cash contribution after promotion and acquisition amortisation. Standalone digital-brandARR multiples are especially poor substitutes for listed-parent cash flow.
