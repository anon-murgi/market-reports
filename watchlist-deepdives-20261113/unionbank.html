UNIONBANK / bank

Union Bank of India

Union Bank’s September quarter combines improving spreads and asset quality with a substantial mismatch between loan and deposit growth. The stock’s near-book valuation looks less demanding than premium private banks, but the sustainability of returns depends on funding discipline and ECL buffers rather than simply extrapolating the present low credit cost.

Research viewPositive operating recovery with a funding constraint to monitor; balance valuation appeal against late-benign asset quality.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • Union’s immediate opportunity is to convert a large funding and distribution franchise into higher-quality earning assets without sacrificing funding economics. Loan growth of 18.53% versus deposit growth of 6.87% makes the source, tenor and cost of new liabilities a central thesis variable.
  • Management cites approximately 120% average LCR, around 84% domestic credit-deposit ratio and longer-tenor FCNR funding. These show available funding tools and current liquidity comfort, but do not demonstrate that incremental asset growth has the same risk-adjusted return as the legacy book.

Forward scenarios · analyst interpretation

BULL
  • NIM remains near 2.8–2.9% while deposits catch up to credit demand.
  • Strong internal capital generation absorbs ECL transition and supports profitable growth.
  • Asset quality remains benign enough for a sustained premium to book.
BASE
  • Loan growth moderates or deposit pricing rises to restore funding balance.
  • RoA remains above 1% but present recoveries and credit costs normalise.
  • Valuation tracks sustainable bank returns rather than the fastest quarterly PAT growth.
BEAR
  • Funding competition erodes NIM as loan growth outruns deposits.
  • ECL or fresh slippages consume more capital than current buffers can absorb.
  • A PSU-bank discount returns if execution or underwriting weakens.

Bank-specific framework

Assess NII, NIM, deposits, asset quality, capital and RoA/RoE. The local consolidated table intentionally has no industrial sales, EBITDA or OPM; those blanks are not operating weakness.

Competitive position

A large public-sector franchise combines branch-led distribution and a broad loan book with state ownership and a lower valuation. Compare return durability and deposit costs rather than applying a uniform PSU discount or private-bank premium.

Macro read-through

A rate increase can lift floating-rate asset yields before deposit repricing, while simultaneously increasing borrower stress and market losses. The earnings benefit is time dependent, not automatic.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

  • Management’s approximate ₹5,500 crore total ECL transition estimate is partly offset by ₹1,000 crore additional buffer. Reverse valuation and sustainable-return constraints; point target withheld

Snapshot valuation is 1.07× book and 7.05× earnings, with snapshot RoE 15.7%. Latest standalone call RoE 17.21% is a different period/scope; do not mix it into an exact justified-P/B model. A valuation near book is attractive only if normalised RoE remains above cost of equity.

  • A full P/ABV requires deduction of unprovided NPAs, ECL and subsidiary valuation reconciliation.
  • The previous arbitrary bear/base/bull target-price sensitivities have been removed. The deeper valuation discussion states the economic return conditions and analyst assumptions instead.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • The dated snapshot P/B is 1.07 x and ROE is 15.7%. A deliberately simplified perpetual-bank identity, P/B=(ROE −g)/(cost of equity −g), using analyst assumptions of 13% cost of equity and 6% long-run growth, would require a steady-state ROE of about 13.5% at that book multiple. This is a diagnostic of the earnings expectations in the price, not a fair value, forecast or calibrated discount rate. A changing growth phase, subsidiary values, adjusted book and surplus capital can materially alter the answer.
  • Union’s approximately 1.07 x snapshot book value is lower than KVB’s 2.40 x and ICICI’s 2.93 x, but those franchises differ in funding mix, sustainable returns and subsidiary value. The discount is an opportunity only if the bank retains a core return above its required equity return through a less benign credit environment. No target P/ABV is supplied without adjusted-book and ECL reconciliation.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Bank of India is a closer public-sector comparison than KVB or ICICI for ownership and broad funding characteristics. Its dated snapshot P/B of 0.74 x and ROE of 12.4% compare with Union’s 1.07 x and 15.7%, suggesting that the market already differentiates return quality. These are dated consolidated snapshot ratios, not a synchronized standalone peer-return study.
