Investment thesis and business cycles

Core and newer businesses all contribute

Cash conversion is the biggest improvement

Management reports moving from ₹775 crore net debt in March to net cash in September. Cash conversion cycle fell from 82 to 44 days, though the CFO says 50–60 days is more sustainable, making some working-capital cash release nonrepeatable.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

Flooring turnaround has operating substance

Flooring EBITDA margin 10.8% follows 10.4% Q1, helped by portfolio/right-sizing and pricing. This reduces a drag, but management guides a steady low-double-digit margin rather than an unlimited growth breakout.

Vapi recovery provides near-term throughput opportunity

Flooding disrupted dispatches and management estimated ₹100–250 crore lost/deferred topline; restoration timing and insurance remain important. US pillow operations add another growth lever with FY29 $80–100 million ambition.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • Home-textile earnings combine manufacturing efficiency, customer-program mix, input-price pass-through and trade exposure. Group growth therefore needs a bridge between meters/units, realizations and new categories. The US onshore pillow strategy offers proximity and lead-time advantages, but 2.4 x quarterly growth is not the same as mature plant economics.
  • The pillow disclosure is especially informative: operating breakeven coexists with Ohio 83% and Nevada 52% utilization. Incremental revenue can absorb fixed costs, but the lower-teen margin ambition should be tested against raw-material, freight, labor and service-level costs at each plant. A blended utilization number hides different plant ramps.

Home textiles and retailer replenishment

Management says 60–70% of US business is replenishment-led. This supports order continuity but is still exposed to retail inventory, household spending and sourcing changes.

Cost and trade sensitivity

Cotton, polymers and energy costs remain elevated; over 90% FOB business reduces direct freight exposure but customer pricing and global demand still matter. Trade access is useful only when it converts to programs and volumes.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

  • Flooring, advanced textiles and US pillow investments should be evaluated on their individual contribution and incremental capital employed. Net-cash status creates optionality but does not itself make a new acquisition or new plant return-accretive.

Sector-appropriate normalized earnings / enterprise value analysis

Normalize cash conversion and cycle earnings before using EV/EBITDA or P/E. Snapshot P/E 51.9 x partly reflects depressed FY26 profit; annualizing the new quarter assumes the recovery persists. Net-cash disclosure needs a precise operating-cash and debt-perimeter bridge before enterprise valuation.

  • 50–60 days is management’s sustainable cash cycle reference, not 44 days forever.
  • Low-teens EBITDA and double-digit revenue growth are retained FY27 targets.
  • Annual capex ₹400–500 crore is current baseline; new opportunities may increase future investment.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • Separate the core home-textile franchise from still-ramping categories and deduct debt/leases on a consistent basis. A recovery multiple applied to group EBITDA can overstate value if low-return growth categories require repeated investment.
  • The bull case requires both normalized cash conversion and new-category profitability; the base case allows some working-capital reversal; the bear case includes trade/input pressure plus delayed Vapi or US plant normalization. Tariff refunds are separately tracked until recognition and customer sharing are clear.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • Indo Count is a closer home-textile/export comparator than a yarn manufacturer, but US utility bedding, brands, capacity footprint and customer programs differ. Trident adds different product/integration exposure. Compare recurring EBITDA, returns on invested capital and working-capital intensity rather than a common textile-sector P/E.
