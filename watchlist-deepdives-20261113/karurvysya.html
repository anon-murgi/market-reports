Karur Vysya Bank Limited

Karur Vysya Bank’s September result strengthens the case that its turnaround has become a high-return banking franchise: PAT grew strongly with very low reported problem loans and robust NIM. At around 2.4× snapshot book, the investment debate has shifted from recovery upside to how long these exceptional returns can persist.

Research viewHigh operating quality in current evidence; require a valuation cushion against normalisation of margins and credit costs.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • KVB’s operating model is weighted to retail, agriculture and commercial lending rather than a very large corporate book: RAM is 86% of advances. The relevant moat is granular underwriting and transaction relationships, with the cost of maintaining deposits and branches paid from loan spreads and fees.
  • The gold-loan book of ₹32,153 crore is about 29% of advances. Management disclosed agri/non-agri LTV of 67%/59% and selective pricing to moderate retail gold growth. Low current delinquencies are useful evidence, but concentration and the collateral-price cycle still need stress testing.

BULL
  • FY27 credit growth reaches 18–20% with 4%+NIM and 2%+RoA.
  • Relationship-led CASA funding allows growth without excessive bulk-deposit dependence.
  • Strong retained earnings allow disciplined expansion while capital remains comfortable.
BASE
  • Growth remains healthy but margins and credit costs normalise from unusually strong levels.
  • Reported PAT continues to compound at a lower pace than the latest YoY comparison.
  • The stock retains a quality premium with limited tolerance for underwriting mistakes.
BEAR
  • SME stress or gold-collateral weakness creates slippages after rapid growth.
  • Deposit competition erodes the 4%+NIM assumption.
  • A lower justified P/B offsets book-value compounding.

Regional franchise, national opportunity

A commercially oriented private bank can compound through SME and retail relationships without matching the breadth of the largest banks. Concentration, underwriting and deposit quality remain the counterweights to strong reported return ratios.

Valuation follows sustainable returns

P/B relative to sustainable RoE and capital requirements is the primary lens. Bank cash-flow statements and industrial ROCE are poor substitutes for capital generation, credit costs and deposit stability.

Macro read-through

A higher rate cycle can support loan yields but tests SME cash flows and deposit pricing. Gold-loan collateral values and commercial borrower stress require monitoring alongside the margin benefit.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

Reverse valuation and sustainable-return constraints; point target withheld

Snapshot P/B 2.4× and P/E 12.5× reflect high current profitability. A higher P/B than PSU peers is justified only if the superior RoA, asset quality and growth survive a less benign credit cycle.

  • No guarantee that current RoA 2.1% or low problem-loan ratios are permanent.
  • No P/ABV target without a full adjusted-book and ECL reconciliation.
  • The previous arbitrary bear/base/bull target-price sensitivities have been removed. The deeper valuation discussion states the economic return conditions and analyst assumptions instead.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • The dated snapshot P/B is 2.40 x and ROE is 19.1%. A deliberately simplified perpetual-bank identity, P/B=(ROE −g)/(cost of equity −g), using analyst assumptions of 13% cost of equity and 6% long-run growth, would require a steady-state ROE of about 22.8% at that book multiple. This is a diagnostic of the earnings expectations in the price, not a fair value, forecast or calibrated discount rate. A changing growth phase, subsidiary values, adjusted book and surplus capital can materially alter the answer.
  • At 2.40 x reported book, a strong quarter is insufficient: the market needs confidence that high ROA converts to durable ROE after a normalized credit charge and funding cost. The model’s indicative 22.8% steady-state ROE requirement exceeds the dated snapshot 19.1%, showing that the premium also depends on a growth phase, lower risk assumptions or other valuation considerations. This is a stress diagnostic, not evidence of a precise overvaluation.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • KVB merits comparison with granular private-sector banks for underwriting, gold/commercial mix and liability costs. Comparing its P/B directly with Union or Bank of India without allowing for different returns and ownership would be crude. Conversely, a private-bank label alone does not justify a premium if core margin compresses or fast growth raises future credit costs.
