Investment thesis and business cycles

Proposed IHCL merger changes the shareholder payoff

The approved proposal exchanges 25 IHCL shares for 117 OHL shares, subject to approvals. Returns depend on the acquirer share price and time to completion as well as hotel performance; the earlier hotel-only framing was incomplete.

Primary source

Taj/IHCL association supports positioning

The official release identifies seven hotels and three Taj-branded properties. Brand access and asset upgrades can support pricing, but the limited portfolio concentrates property and local-market risk.

Historical cash flow provides a funding base

FY26 CFO ₹128 crore and FCF ₹93 crore exceeded annual PAT ₹68 crore. This is encouraging historical evidence; upgrades and renovation outlays must be deducted before assuming a distributable cash yield.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • The operating business earns room and ancillary revenue from a concentrated South Indian portfolio supported by IHCL brands. RevPAR growth combines occupancy and rates; revenue growth alone cannot isolate either. Renovation closures, food and beverage activity, and property mix affect how room productivity reaches cash profit.
  • The shareholder case differs from an unchanged standalone hotel case. The proposed exchange makes both acquirer performance and transaction conditions material. The hotels and strategic investments also support a fallback valuation if implementation does not occur.

Hotel economics

Revenue equals available rooms, occupancy, room rate and ancillary spending. RevPAR growth is useful, but occupancy versus ADR and renovation closures determine how it reaches profit.

Regional concentration and seasonality

The seven-hotel portfolio is concentrated in South India, so property-level upgrades, corporate travel, leisure traffic and wedding calendars can create material quarterly variation.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

Conditional exchange-parity analysis plus standalone hotel/NAV fallback

The proposed all-stock exchange makes IHCL price exposure and completion risk central. At matching 12 November 2026 clean closes, gross parity is ₹188.46 versus OHL ₹173.90. This is neither a cash offer nor risk-free arbitrage. Value the failed-deal case using hotel operating cash flow, investments and funding separately.

  • The proposed ratio of 25/117 is not a completed exchange.
  • Local 12 November closes were IHCL ₹882 and OHL ₹173.90. Gross parity assumes an unchanged IHCL price, successful completion and no costs.
  • Approval timing, fractional entitlements, dividends, corporate actions and hedging or financing costs can affect the realized payoff.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • Gross parity equals the acquirer price multiplied by the exchange ratio. A discount may compensate for time, approval risk, failed-deal downside, dividends, transaction costs and inability to hedge. A larger spread is not free profit.
  • The failed-deal case should value hotel operations and financial interests net of appropriate liabilities. Transaction parity cannot be used as both successful-deal value and failed-deal valuation.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • IHCL is the relevant payoff reference under the exchange proposal. EIH, Chalet and other hotel owners remain operating/NAV comparators, with different ownership and lease profiles. A broad hotel P/E ranking would miss the announced transaction.
