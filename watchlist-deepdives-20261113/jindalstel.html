Investment thesis and business cycles

Existing asset ramp offers meaningful volume growth

Q2 sales were 2.38 million tonnes and management reiterates roughly 10.5–11 million tonne FY27 ambition, with H2 output near 6 million tonnes. That asks for roughly 0.9–0.96 million tonne monthly saleable/production run rates, so operating execution is the key evidence.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

Slurry pipeline now has a concrete operating milestone

The 192 km Barbil–Angul pipeline is commissioned and first slurry received, with continuous operation expected after October 23. Management estimates ₹700/tonne freight saving on relevant transported ore, not automatically on every tonne of steel sold.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

Product mix and raw-material integration can improve economics

Management targets 65% flats including plates, rather than endorsing an analyst 70% mix. Captive coal coverage is about 50–55% currently, with higher coverage sought; cost competitiveness must be measured after ramp, not assumed.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

Financial discipline is improving

Call net debt fell ₹1,141 crore sequentially to ₹14,786 crore, net debt/EBITDA 1.51 x. FY26 free cash flow was negative ₹2,343 crore and annual growth capex guidance remains ₹7,500–10,000 crore, so cash discipline stays central.

September 2026 local financials and current company earnings-call transcript; source links below

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • The core question is cash EBITDA per sold tonne after higher ore, coking-coal, energy and freight costs. Record 2.38 mt sales is valuable, yet sequential PAT declined from ₹844 cr to ₹771 cr. Volume growth is therefore not evidence that per-unit profitability has inflected upward.
  • The slurry pipeline has an identifiable physical driver. At the discussed 20,000 tonnes of ore/day and ₹700/tonne freight saving, a 365-day upper arithmetic run-rate is roughly ₹511 cr annually. This is an illustrative gross cost saving, not management-guaranteed PAT: actual utilization, maintenance, downtime, other logistics, depreciation and tax matter. It must not be calculated as ₹700 times every tonne of steel sales.

  • Pipeline freight savings and captive resource use expand EBITDA per tonne.
  • Cash earnings fund capex while leverage falls below the 1.5 x through-cycle aim.

Steel spread and cost curve

Domestic steel demand and a higher plate/flat mix help realizations; coking coal, power and freight determine conversion to EBITDA. Captive resources provide resilience but do not remove cost opportunity trade-offs.

Export and policy optionality

Management expresses optimism on EU access and trade negotiations while acknowledging CBAM. These are conditional opportunities and are not modeled as secured sales or duty savings.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

Sector-appropriate normalized earnings / enterprise value analysis

Use normalized EV/EBITDA and mid-cycle return on invested capital, with a bridge from current capex to commissioned volume and savings. Snapshot P/E 36.8 x should not be valued as a stable compounder multiple; neither peak steel prices nor full capacity should be assumed by default.

  • ₹700/tonne pipeline saving applies to relevant ore logistics, not all sales without a volume bridge.
  • FY27 production/sales targets are not identical; management uses different gross/net figures.
  • Annual growth capex ₹7,500–10,000 crore remains the disclosed funding framework.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • Value normalized steel EBITDA and separately identified mining/subsidiary economics without double counting. Do not assign a compounder multiple to a peak spread or value all recently installed capacity at steady state while omitting its depreciation and working-capital needs.
  • The bull case requires sales volume, saleable mix and savings together; the base case permits coal cost increases and gradual ramp; the bear case includes delayed cost benefits plus capex-funded debt. The dated 36.8 x snapshot P/E says little by itself about mid-cycle earnings value.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • JSW Steel is a scale/capex comparator but has different consolidation/JV and raw-material exposures. Tata Steel’s international businesses and SAIL’s operating structure introduce further differences. Compare EBITDA/tonne and free cash flow after matching domestic versus consolidated perimeters; headline P/E comparisons are weak.
