ICICI Bank’s September result supports strong franchise quality, but spectacular balance-sheet growth is partly inflated by FCNR-linked flows. The relevant evidence is resilient core operating profit and low net problem loans alongside a softer margin; the premium book multiple needs sustained risk-adjusted returns rather than the headline 30% loan-growth number.

Research viewQuality franchise at a meaningful valuation premium; prefer disciplined return expectations and explicit funding normalisation.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • The bank’s opportunity is to deepen primary relationships across retail, business banking and corporate ecosystems while optimizing sustainable pre-tax profit. FCNR funding provides medium-term balance-sheet capacity, but the associated matched overseas leverage is not economically identical to the domestic retail book.
  • Excluding FCNR, loans still grew 24% and deposits 17%; that is a strong underlying outcome. The important question is what return the incremental balance sheet earns after funding, operating cost, capital use and credit losses, rather than whether the reported loan balance grew 30%.

Premium banking franchise lens

An integrated retail, SME and corporate bank can create value through wallet share and primary relationships. Compare risk-adjusted PBT, funding quality and returns; raw loan growth is insufficient.

FCNR activity changes both growth and spread ratios

The special deposit programme adds matched balance-sheet activity at lower spreads. Its NIM dilution does not automatically mean weaker absolute earnings, but the associated growth should not be valued like higher-return retail lending.

Macro read-through

Tighter policy can benefit floating loan yields but raises liability costs and borrower stress. Treasury and FX movements also affect consolidated accounts and must be distinguished from the bank’s core earnings.

Macro sensitivities

Policy rates, inflation, funding conditions and household demand affect these businesses through different channels. The company-specific implications are discussed in the business, valuation and risk sections.

Reverse valuation and sustainable-return constraints; point target withheld

  • No forward book growth, probability or target price implied.

  • The previous arbitrary bear/base/bull target-price sensitivities have been removed. The deeper valuation discussion states the economic return conditions and analyst assumptions instead.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • The dated snapshot P/B is 2.93 x and ROE is 15.9%. A deliberately simplified perpetual-bank identity, P/B=(ROE −g)/(cost of equity −g), using analyst assumptions of 13% cost of equity and 6% long-run growth, would require a steady-state ROE of about 26.5% at that book multiple. This is a diagnostic of the earnings expectations in the price, not a fair value, forecast or calibrated discount rate. A changing growth phase, subsidiary values, adjusted book and surplus capital can materially alter the answer.
  • Applying this perpetual formula directly to ICICI’s consolidated book would be particularly incomplete because listed/unlisted subsidiaries and a multi-year growth phase can create value outside a one-stage bank-return model. The diagnostic highlights that a 2.93 x book premium requires more than mechanically capitalizing the current 15.9% consolidated snapshot ROE. A proper SOTP must avoid adding subsidiary values on top of consolidated earnings/book without eliminating double counting.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • KVB is useful as a granular-loan profitability comparison but has different scale and concentration; Union/BOI offer much lower book multiples with different normalized returns. HDFC Bank and Axis Bank are closer large private-bank franchise comparisons. The decisive peer variables are sustainable ROE, deposit acquisition cost, risk-adjusted growth and subsidiary treatment, not raw credit growth or current NNPA alone.
