Cemindia Projects Limited

Cemindia’s ₹32,715 crore order book provides multi-year execution visibility, but the current quarter shows the cost of that growth: operating margins are not expanding and new equity/capex will dilute near-term return ratios. The ₹1,400 crore QIP turns the thesis into a capital-productivity test as much as an order-inflow story.

Research viewStrong order visibility; wait for evidence that larger, more mechanized contracts improve per-share cash earnings after dilution.

Capital access supports bigger projects, with dilution

The completed ₹1,400 crore QIP issued 1.26 crore shares at ₹1,108. Management accepts near-term ROE compression while large machines wait for deployment; the additional ₹5,000 crore authorization is enabling, not a completed issue.

Group opportunity is useful but must be competitively won

Management explicitly denies automatic award privileges from Adani entities. Its ₹50,000 crore addressable group opportunity is potential pipeline, not assured revenue.

How the business earns: economics and competitive position

  • Cemindia earns a project margin across engineering, procurement, labor/equipment and execution risk. Backlog indicates potential future work, but revenue recognition depends on mobilization, physical execution and certification; cash collection may lag reported profit. Data-centre electromechanical content is a large revenue component rather than an automatic premium-margin category.
  • The scale change also changes funding needs. A96-day working-capital metric and equipment mobilization can absorb a substantial part of new orders’ apparent economic benefit. Group-related projects contribute visibility but also concentrate order sourcing and counterparties.

Marine and underground work reward execution equipment and technical capability; order size alone does not establish return quality. Certification, advances, equipment deployment and cash collection matter.

Group and external pipeline

Adani ownership expands opportunity access, while competitive bidding and related-party economics remain important. Avoid valuing the ₹1.3 lakh crore external pipeline as backlog.

Capital allocation and cash requirements

  • The ₹1,400 cr QIP must be assessed by deployment return, cash absorption and dilution. A larger equity base can lower ROE before revenue catches up; this is not merely an accounting issue because investors have supplied additional capital that needs to earn an acceptable return. Exact backlog cut-off remains an open item; it should not be assumed from the October order discussion alone.
  • Normal H2 capex and the large-TBM contingency are different cases. A major equipment programme requires contract visibility, utilization, mobilization economics, depreciation and exit value. Do not assume the whole ₹1,500 cr conditional amount is committed immediately, or exclude it when constructing a growth case that depends on the associated work.

Sector-appropriate normalized earnings / enterprise value analysis

Use post-QIP diluted earnings and normalized EPC cash conversion; cross-check EV/EBITDA against specialist EPC peers. Snapshot P/E 39.1 x and ROCE 32.8% are historical starting points, not guaranteed post-expansion returns. A target before a full diluted share/capex bridge would be misleading.

  • ₹1,400 crore QIP is completed; ₹5,000 crore additional enabling authorization is not a committed issuance.
  • September debt/net worth precede the October QIP effect.
  • Data-center revenue does not imply software-style margins: management says do not expect above 10% in that work.

Valuation logic and what the current price requires

  • Use normalized project earnings and cash conversion after QIP dilution, with contract-specific capital needs. A backlog multiple is insufficient: specialized tunnels/marine projects and large procurement-heavy data-centre packages can have different margin and capital intensity.
  • The bull case requires execution and adequate equipment utilization; the base case assumes margin around management’s 10% guide and staggered capex; the bear case includes delayed certification/collections, customer concentration and a low-return enlarged equity base. A price target needs post-issue share count and a funded return bridge.

Which peers are economically comparable

  • L&T is diversified across EPC, manufacturing, services and finance; NCC has different project mix and customer exposures; specialized tunneling contractors carry different equipment risks. Peer valuation should be adjusted for net cash/debt, working-capital intensity, contract risk and the share of group/customer concentration.
