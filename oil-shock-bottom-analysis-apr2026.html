The Fiscal Tightrope — The Sleeper Risk

The risk most investors are underweighting. The government's excise whipsaw — cut ₹10-13/L on Mar 27, raised ₹2/L on Apr 8 — tells you how tight the math is.

The Cruel Triple Bind

No Good Options
Option 1: Cut excise → fiscal deficit widens → bond yields rise → borrowing costs up → crowds out investment
Option 2: Pass through fuel costs → inflation spikes → RBI tightens → consumer demand collapses
Option 3: Maintain deficit target → cut capex → kills investment-led growth thesis

Elara explicitly warns: "If the government maintains 4.3%, it may have to cut long-term infrastructure spending used to boost growth and jobs."

Most likely outcome: Mix of all three — partial pass-through, partial excise sacrifice, some capex deferral. Fiscal deficit at 4.5-4.8% vs 4.3% target.

Bernstein "GFC Moment" — Full Anatomy

Bernstein/SocGen (Mar 28, 2026) — the most bearish institutional report. Their framework and what it means.

Core warning: In the 2008 GFC, India's growth slipped from ~10% to 5%, inflation spiked to 10%, rupee depreciated 30%. Bernstein frames the current crisis as a potential repeat: "India's bright story on the global stage had one common denominator — crude staying exceptionally low." Between 2014-2021, crude was largely below $80. That structural tailwind has now reversed.
