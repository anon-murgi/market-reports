1The idea

What is a golden cross?

Two moving averages, one fast and one slow, and the moment the fast one climbs over the slow one.

A moving average smooths a price chart. The 50-day average (SMA50) is the mean closing price of the last 50 trading days; it tracks the recent trend. The 200-day average (SMA200) covers roughly a year and moves slowly.

A golden cross happens on the day the 50-day line rises above the 200-day line. Traders read it as "the short-term trend has turned up and is now stronger than the long-term trend". The opposite event, the 50-day falling below the 200-day, is a death cross.

This study added one safety check. A raw cross often flickers back and forth, so a signal only qualifies once the 50-day line has stayed at least 1% above the 200-day line for 10 closes in a row.

In plain words

The question this whole study asks: if you buy a stock when its golden cross qualifies, and sell by fixed rules, do you beat simply owning the stocks?

In plain words

On the Nifty, a year after a death cross looked almost as good as a year after a golden cross, so most of the "golden cross works" claims online measure the market's normal upward drift. On individual stocks, a golden cross day was a slightly worse time to buy than an average day in the same year, by about 1.4 to 1.5 percentage points.

4Diagnosis

Where the signal lost money

Phase 1 split 4,455 clean-price signals by what was knowable on the signal day, then asked which groups dragged the average down.

63-day excess return by group, in percentage points

Excess = signal return minus the same stock's average return that year. Blue beat the base; red lost to it. Bar labels show how many signals fall in each group.

In plain words

The worst group by far: stocks that had already run more than 25% above their 200-day average by the time the signal qualified. Those 760 signals lost 9.6 points each against the base. Buying late into a big move was the main leak. Grade C signals (the weaker ones) and signals with a very wide gap between the averages also did badly.

The four fixes, frozen before testing

Each fix came from the diagnosis above and was written into config.json before any portfolio result existed. Writing them down first stops anyone from quietly picking whichever rule happened to work.

5The trading system

How the v1.2 trading system works

The earlier tests looked at signals one at a time. v1.2 runs a real account day by day, with limited cash, a four-stock limit and real costs.

STEP 1
Spot the signal

After the close, find stocks whose 50-day line crossed the 200-day and then stayed 1% above it for 10 closes.

STEP 2
Check eligibility

At least 400 days of price history, listed 2+ years, and yearly volatility between 25% and 70%.

STEP 3
Grade it A or C

A: candle fully above both lines, one line rising over 21 days, price no more than 25% above the 200-day. Everything else is C.

STEP 4
Buy next morning

Buy at the next day's open. Grade A ranks first. Hold at most 4 stocks. No borrowing, whole shares only.

STEP 5
Size by risk

Weight = 15% ÷ the stock's volatility, capped at 25% of the account. Grade C gets a quarter of that.

STEP 6
Set a fixed stop

Stop = entry price − 3 × ATR (average daily range over 14 days). It never moves up.

STEP 7
Exit

Sell on the stop, on a death cross that holds 10 days, or after 63 trading days. H3 and Combined also sell after 2 closes below the 50-day line.

STEP 8
Pay costs

STT 0.1% each side, stamp duty, exchange and SEBI fees, brokerage, GST, plus 0.04% slippage each side. Idle cash earns 0%.

    11Quality checks

    Can we trust these numbers?

    The arithmetic is solid. The data has known limits, and they all flatter the strategy.

    What passed

    • 20 of 20 independent ledger checks passed across 77,792 fills and 87,912 daily account rows.
    • A separate script rebuilt every daily cash balance from the trades. Largest error: under ₹0.000001.
    • All 7,517 signal buys happened exactly one (or, for the delay test, two) market days after the signal. No trade used future prices.
    • No stop-loss level ever moved after entry.
    • All 503 input files were copied and fingerprinted (SHA-256) before any result was computed.

    Known limits

    • Survivorship bias. The 496 stocks are names still around in 2026. Companies that collapsed or delisted are missing, which makes both the system and the basket look better than reality.
    • Validation was not blind. Earlier signal studies had already examined 2016 to 2021.
    • Today's fee schedule applied to all years, and slippage is a flat guess.
    • Liquidity unverified. Volume data is a rough proxy, so real fills at size could be worse.
    • Eight names had no clean price file, including GSPL, HEG, ICICIPRULI and JBCHEPHARM.
    12Conclusion

    Conclusion

    ✕ REJECT

    The golden cross, with every fix tried here, does not beat owning the same stocks equally.

    The research is closed on this data. More tuning on 2007 to 2026 would only fit noise, because every year of it has been seen.

    The case, in five lines

      What would reopen the question

      • Historical Nifty 500 membership by date, including delisted and suspended companies.
      • Point-in-time market caps, sectors and traded value, so the size and liquidity rules can run.
      • A dated fee schedule and a real market-impact model.
      • A future period nobody has looked at, tested once with the rules frozen in advance.

      What the golden cross is good for

      Across the literature, the Nifty test and this backtest, one result repeats: trend rules cut drawdowns. The Combined version's worst fall was less than half the basket's. Anyone who values a smaller fall more than the higher return could study it as a risk overlay, though that is a different question from the one tested here, and this page does not answer it.

      13Reference

      Glossary

      SMA50 / SMA200
      Simple moving average: the mean closing price over the last 50 or 200 trading days.
      Golden / death cross
      SMA50 moving above / below SMA200.
      Qualified signal
      A golden cross where SMA50 then stays at least 1% above SMA200 for 10 closes in a row.
      OR rule
      Grade A needs either the 50-day or the 200-day line to be rising over 21 days. "Only-fast" signals are those where only the 50-day line rises.
      Extension
      How far the close sits above the 200-day average, as a percentage.
      ATR
      Average true range: the typical daily high-to-low move over 14 days. Used to set the stop distance.
      CAGR
      Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly rate that links start and end values.
      Drawdown
      The fall from the highest account value so far to a later low.
      Turnover
      Total value traded in a year divided by the account's value.
      Excess return
      Signal return minus the return of the same stock on an average day that year.
      Slippage
      The gap between the price you expect and the price you get when trading.
      Survivorship bias
      Testing only on companies that survived to today, which hides the failures.
      Block bootstrap
      Re-running the comparison many times on randomly reshuffled whole years to see how much the answer could vary by luck.
      Holdout
      A slice of data kept unseen until one final test.
